# Strategy Ecosystem Mapper

Generates Corporate Forest ecosystem maps using intuitive nature metaphors to make strategy accessible and enable meaningful participation from all stakeholders.

## Core Principle

**Strategy becomes participatory when articulated as an ecosystem.** The Corporate Forest metaphor translates abstract competitive dynamics into concrete, relatable imagery that anyone can engage with.

## When to Use This Skill

- User needs to understand competitive landscape or market positioning
- Strategic planning sessions where diverse stakeholders must contribute
- Market entry or expansion decisions
- Competitive response planning
- **Even if** user doesn't say "ecosystem" but asks about competitors, market position, or strategic opportunities

## Input Requirements

Accepts input in any format:
- Company/organization description
- Market or industry context
- Known competitors (even partial list)
- Customer segments served
- Current challenges or strategic questions

If input lacks detail, ask clarifying questions about:
1. What organization are we analyzing?
2. What market/industry do you operate in?
3. Who do you consider your main competitors?
4. What customers do you serve?
5. What strategic question are you trying to answer?

## Processing Steps

### Step 1: Map the Corporate Forest Layers

Apply the Corporate Forest metaphor to categorize all players:

#### Companies (Trees)
- **Giant Oak:** Dominant incumbent controlling the market (e.g., Amazon, Microsoft in their domains)
- **Fast Redwood:** Rising challenger growing rapidly, threatening incumbents (e.g., Tesla, TikTok in emergence)
- **Maple Tree:** Adaptable player changing strategies seasonally based on conditions
- **Dogwood:** Specialized niche player with premium offerings, small but profitable
- **Wildflowers:** Fast-growing startups (most die, few survive to become trees)
- **Ferns:** Service providers, consultants, enablers of the ecosystem

#### Relationships (Animals & Organisms)
- **Bees:** Loyal customers who pollinate/spread word through referrals
- **Birds:** Big customers who can carry you to new markets
- **Mushrooms:** Investors/VCs (feed on dead companies, help new growth)

#### Disruptors (Forces)
- **Invasive Kudzu:** Disruptors that change everything, smothering existing players
- **Fire:** Economic crashes, major disruptions, regulatory shifts
- **Strangler Fig:** Platform businesses (starts small, eventually dominates host)

#### Resources (Environment)
- **Sunlight (Revenue):** Customer attention, market share, mindshare
- **Rainfall (Market Awareness):** Brand visibility, distribution reach
- **Soil Quality:** Talent pool, capital availability, strategic assets

### Step 2: Position Your Organization

Answer these questions to establish position:

1. **What type of tree are you?** (Oak, Redwood, Maple, Dogwood, Wildflower)
2. **Where are you in the forest canopy?** (Dominant, emerging, mid-level, understory)
3. **What relationships sustain you?** (Which bees, birds, mushrooms?)
4. **What resources do you depend on?** (Sunlight, rainfall, soil quality)
5. **What disruptors threaten you?** (Kudzu, fire, strangler figs)
6. **What's your growth trajectory?** (Growing, stable, declining, pivoting)

### Step 3: Analyze Competitive Dynamics

Examine the ecosystem for:

**Canopy Battles:** Where are giants competing for dominance?
**Understory Opportunities:** What niches are underserved?
**Resource Flows:** Where is sunlight/rainfall concentrated vs. scarce?
**Growth Patterns:** Which species are thriving vs. struggling?
**Symbiotic Relationships:** What partnerships create mutual benefit?
**Predatory Dynamics:** Who is consuming whom?

### Step 4: Identify Strategic Leverage Points

Based on the ecosystem map, identify:

**Positioning Opportunities:**
- Underserved niches (forest floor opportunities)
- Canopy gaps (market leadership vacuums)
- Relationship arbitrage (connecting unconnected parties)

**Resource Strategies:**
- How to capture more sunlight (revenue streams)
- How to access better rainfall (distribution channels)
- How to improve soil quality (talent, capital, assets)

**Defensive Positions:**
- What threats require immediate attention
- What relationships need strengthening
- What capabilities must be built

### Step 5: Generate Strategic Questions

Use the ecosystem to prompt strategic thinking:

1. If we're a [tree type], what would it take to become a [different tree type]?
2. What bees (loyal customers) could we cultivate more intentionally?
3. Are there birds (big customers) we're not serving that could transform our reach?
4. What kudzu (disruptors) should we be watching?
5. Could we become a strangler fig by building a platform?
6. Where is sunlight abundant but competition low?
7. What would happen if [giant oak] decided to enter our space?

## Key Principles

1. **Metaphor Enables Participation:** Anyone can engage with "what tree are we?" vs. "what's your strategic archetype?"
2. **Dynamic Not Static:** Ecosystems change; update the map regularly
3. **Multiple Perspectives:** Different stakeholders may see different forests - explore variations
4. **Action-Oriented:** Mapping is means to decisions, not an end
5. **Honest Assessment:** Don't flatter - call out real positions and threats

## Common Patterns

### Pattern: "We're Special"
When organizations resist honest positioning:
- Ask "What would a neutral observer say?"
- Compare metrics objectively (market share, growth rate, profitability)
- Use customer language: "How do customers describe you vs. competitors?"

### Pattern: "Too Many Giants"
When everything seems threatening:
- Distinguish between "big" and "relevant to us"
- Identify which giants actually compete for your customers
- Look for asymmetries: what can you do they can't/won't?

### Pattern: "No Clear Niche"
When position feels undefined:
- Map customer segments more granularly
- Look for underserved combinations of needs
- Consider partnership positions (fern to someone else's tree)

### Pattern: "Disruption Paranoia"
When every startup seems like kudzu:
- Assess actual threat: do they serve your customers?
- Look for partnership/acquisition opportunities
- Focus on defensible relationships over defensive postures

## Quality Checks

Before delivering output, verify:
- [ ] Every player is categorized with clear rationale
- [ ] Organization's position is honest and specific
- [ ] Relationships (bees, birds, mushrooms) are named concretely
- [ ] At least 3 strategic leverage points identified
- [ ] Strategic questions are thought-provoking, not generic
- [ ] Output enables specific decisions or conversations

## Examples

### Example Input
```
We're a B2B SaaS company providing project management software for creative agencies.
About 50 employees, $8M ARR, growing 40% YoY. 
Competitors include Asana, Monday.com, ClickUp, and some smaller tools like Teamwork.
We focus specifically on agency workflows - client proofing, time tracking, resource planning.
Most customers are agencies with 10-100 employees.
```

### Example Output (Abbreviated)
```markdown

### Forest Overview
Dense canopy dominated by generalist PM platforms, with specialized undergrowth serving niche segments. Sunlight (agency budgets) is abundant but fragmented. Rapid growth attracting new wildflowers continuously.

### Key Players

#### Giants (Oak Trees)
- **Asana/Monday.com:** Dominant generalists capturing broad PM market. Massive sunlight (revenue), extensive rainfall (brand awareness). Not agency-specific but serve many agencies as default choice.
- **Microsoft (with Teams/Planner):** Sleeping giant with enterprise canopy control. Could dominate if focused on agency segment.

#### Challengers (Redwoods)  
- **ClickUp:** Fast-growing, feature-dense platform climbing rapidly. Aggressive pricing, expanding canopy quickly. Threatening mid-market.

#### Niche Players (Dogwoods)
- **[Your Company]:** Specialized for creative agencies with workflow-specific features (proofing, agency time tracking). Defensible position through domain expertise.
- **Teamwork:** Similar agency focus, slightly larger but slower growth.

#### Emerging (Wildflowers)
- Multiple AI-powered scheduling tools
- Vertical-specific solutions (marketing agencies, design studios)
- Most will die; watch for those gaining bee (customer) traction

#### Enablers (Ferns)
- Agency consultants recommending tools
- Integration partners (QuickBooks, Slack, Adobe)
- Implementation specialists

### Your Organization's Position

**Current Identity:** Dogwood (specialized niche player)
**Canopy Level:** Understory - profitable but not dominant
**Key Relationships:**
- Bees: 200+ loyal agency customers providing referrals
- Birds: None significant yet - no enterprise carriers
- Mushrooms: Series A investors supporting growth
**Resource Dependencies:**
- Sunlight: $8M ARR from SMB/mid-market agencies
- Rainfall: Content marketing, word-of-mouth, agency communities
- Soil: Deep agency workflow expertise, product differentiation
**Primary Threats:** 
- Kudzu: AI-native PM tools could reshape category
- Fire: Economic downturn reducing agency software budgets

### Competitive Dynamics

**Canopy Battles:** Asana vs. Monday vs. ClickUp fighting for general PM dominance - burning cash on features and marketing.
**Understory Opportunities:** Agency-specific workflows too narrow for giants but perfect for specialized players.
**Resource Concentrations:** Enterprise budgets (large agencies, holding companies) underserved by current players.
**Emerging Patterns:** AI automation threatening basic PM features; workflow intelligence becoming differentiator.

### Strategic Leverage Points

#### Positioning Opportunities
1. **Own "Agency OS":** Expand from PM to full agency operations platform (proposals, resourcing, financials)
2. **Enterprise Beachhead:** Target top 50 agency networks - could transform you from dogwood to redwood
3. **AI-Native Workflow:** Leapfrog competitors with AI-first approach to agency work

#### Resource Strategies
1. **Cultivate Birds:** Partner with agency holding companies to access portfolio agencies
2. **Deepen Soil:** Acquire smaller specialty tools to broaden capability moat
3. **Increase Rainfall:** Launch agency benchmark reports to become category thought leader

#### Defensive Priorities
1. Monitor AI-native startups closely - acquire or partner before they scale
2. Strengthen bee relationships with customer advisory board
3. Build integration partnerships that increase switching costs

### Strategic Questions for Discussion
1. If we're a Dogwood today, what would it take to become a Redwood in 3 years?
2. Which bird (enterprise customer) could carry us to a new canopy level?
3. Should we fear or embrace AI kudzu - could we become the strangler fig?
4. What would happen if Monday.com launched an "Agencies Edition"?
5. Are we serving the right slice of agencies - should we move upmarket or double down on SMB?

### Recommended Next Steps
1. Interview 10 lost prospects to understand competitive losses
2. Map top 20 agency networks as potential bird relationships
3. Build AI capability assessment - build, buy, or partner?
4. Facilitate leadership workshop using this ecosystem map
```
